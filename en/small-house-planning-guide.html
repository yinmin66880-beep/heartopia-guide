Heartopia Small House Planning Guide: Layout Before Decoration

Plan an original compact home with a clear walking route, useful furniture zones, a restrained palette, and a staged material checklist you can fill in yourself.

Heartopia Guide · Editorial review 2026-10-17

Start here

  1. Choose the three activities your home should support before choosing a style.
  2. Test a simple empty shell and a clear route from the entrance to the furniture you use.
  3. Count materials from the pieces available in your own build menu; this guide provides no invented costs.

1. Write a brief for your own home

Start with a short sentence such as: ‘A compact retreat for cooking, relaxing, and displaying a few favourite catches.’ Pick three priorities. A house designed for social visits will need a different arrangement from one built mainly for screenshots or a personal collection.

Choose one main visual direction and two supporting colours. A warm base, a wood-like accent, and a muted green detail are one possible palette, not a requirement to own specific items. Use the furniture you have and the functions you value; there is no need to duplicate someone else's locked or seasonal set.

2. Plan a route before dividing the rooms

Sketch the entrance first, then the places you will interact with most. Keep a continuous route between them. Put tall or visually heavy pieces where they will not hide the entrance or dominate every view. If the space feels crowded, remove a partition or reduce the display area before enlarging the whole house.

3. Test the shell with temporary furniture

Build the simplest version you can test. Place the essential functional pieces first, then walk from the entrance to each one and try the interactions. Test camera angles from the entrance and the opposite corner. Check doorways and tight corners before filling them with decorations.

Use temporary pieces to test whether a zone is large enough; they do not need to match your final style. When a layout works without decoration, you can invest in finishes with more confidence. Capture your own progress images so that you can compare revisions without borrowing a creator's design.

For each selected piece, record the quantity, material requirement, stock on hand, and remaining amount. Leave fields blank until verified. A prettier inspiration image is not evidence of an affordable material list or a currently obtainable item.

5. Use light and empty space deliberately

Look at the room in different lighting conditions available in your game. Adjust bright accents so the area you use most remains easy to read. Try leaving one surface simple: a room does not need an object in every corner to feel finished.

Make the display area smaller than the main activity zone if it keeps the route clear. Repeat a material or colour in a few places for cohesion, then stop and inspect the room as a whole. These are design suggestions, not claims about an in-game beauty score.

6. Review before gathering for the next stage

  • The home supports the three activities in my brief.
  • I can reach and use essential furniture without obstructions.
  • The entrance and camera views are comfortable.
  • The palette works with pieces I actually own or can obtain.
  • Material quantities came from my build menu, not an estimated screenshot.
  • Any shared reference is credited; I have permission for images I publish.
